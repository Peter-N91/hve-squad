Integration Guide

Squad Connector

Keep your own agents, skills, and execution boundaries. Register a capability manifest and delegate bounded tasks to your external coordinator through the Squad Connector shim, without migrating anything into this package.

Consumer walkthrough: your researcher and implementation coordinator

You already have a squad. Your organization has a research agent with its own skills and an implementation coordinator that runs your coding and validation workflow. This example makes them the automatic researcher and developer endpoints, then adds a .NET practices or GCP specialist without replacing another member.

Use a Squad package supporting connector contract 1.1 and your existing agent host/adapter. The adapter runs your agents and exchanges request/result files with HVE. HVE's Coordinator, Federation Coordinator, Scribe, and council remain unchanged. Your implementation coordinator is a workflow behind the developer role, not the HVE Coordinator.

Step 4: Run your squad normally

Start autopilot with saved defaults
/squad mode=autopilot Implement the approved validation change and its regression tests. Do not deploy or push.

No execution= flag is needed. Research uses your research agent; planning stays with the existing lead; implementation uses your coordinator's workflow; HVE's closing review and applicable council/risk gates still apply. Other ordinary work roles support the same mechanism.

Your adapter must be running to handle the requests. With file-drop transport, a result that is not ready leaves the stage awaiting, never a silent fallback to the built-in worker. Consumer automation can resume it. For a manual handoff, continue the same task:

Resume a pending exchange, if needed
/squad mode=autopilot Continue the current run. Check the pending connector result using its existing taskId and binding; do not create a new task.

The connector supplies neither an external runner nor an automatic cross-process wake-up service.

What is the Squad Connector?

The Squad Connector is a file-based integration contract that lets you bring your own coordinator (or single agent) into HVE Squad's methodology. You register a single capability manifest describing what your coordinator can do, where it lives on disk, and which squad roles can delegate work to it. When a dispatched role needs external work done, the Squad Connector shim writes a delegation-request to your manifest's drop path. Your own coordinator (running entirely outside this package) reads it, does the work, and writes back a result envelope with the deliverable. The shim validates and reports the outcome, exactly as it reports any other role's result.

You keep your own agents, skills, and execution boundaries. Nothing inside this package runs or installs your coordinator code. No migration of your tools, no fork of your methodology. The connector is a shim that hands work across a boundary and brings back results — nothing more.

No blocking, no MCP, no live transport. The connector implements Mode 2: human-in-the-loop file drop. The shim writes the delegation-request and returns immediately; your coordinator runs on your own schedule (by file watcher, cron job, human hand, or whatever you prefer), and the shim picks up the result on a later dispatch. This is the only transport mode supported today.

Architectural fit: External Cast

Connector role modes are roster-owned. During registration/build/refresh, consumers add entries under Connector Bindings and choose whether each binding replaces an existing execution surface or adds a new connector-backed team member. The endpoint behind a binding may be a single external agent or an external orchestrator.

Replacement retains the original internal member. Request-time execution can choose original, a named connector:<binding-id>, or safe both where output roots are collision-free. Interactive clarification happens after ordinary role classification when choice remains ambiguous. In unattended/autopilot paths, unresolved ambiguity is fail-closed: stop and escalate rather than guessing.

Multiple additive connector members are supported. Example: an Azure squad can add a connector-backed gcp architecture assessor aligned to azure-architect. That alignment grants routing and deliverable compatibility only. It does not grant Azure capability by implication, and it does not grant any council lens membership.

Governance shell and council stay internal. Even when a binding targets a consumer-owned orchestrator endpoint for a work role, the HVE Squad Coordinator remains the governance shell. External orchestrators cannot own gates, council verdicts, approvals, squad state writes, or Scribe authority. Council membership remains canonical/internal and connector roles never extend or replace it.

The seat model: your agents, HVE governance

HVE runs the method; your agents perform the bounded work. Think of a role as a seat: keep the HVE agent, select your own agent with its skills, or put your entire workflow behind that seat. Your implementation workflow does not replace the squad's Research → Plan → Implement → Review cycle.

The capability manifest

You author one JSON file, the capability manifest, that describes your connector. The squad never executes this file; it reads it as data to validate and route work.

Field Type Required Meaning
connectorId string yes Stable identifier for this connector, used to bind every request and result to it. Pick something like acme-coordinator-v2.
schemaVersion string yes The contract version this manifest was authored against. Use "1.0" for legacy single-role flow, "1.1" for role modes.
transport string yes The invocation mode. Must be "file" (no other value is supported). "mcp" is reserved for a possible future transport; do not use it.
endpointKind string 1.1: yes
1.0: omit		 What is behind the connector boundary: "agent" or "orchestrator".
capabilities array of string yes Descriptive prose of what your coordinator can do. Not executable. Example: "Implements a bounded development task against our in-house coding standard and returns changed files."
roles array of string yes Which squad role IDs this connector may be registered against. Example: ["developer", "reviewer"]. Consult the roster catalog for valid role IDs.
dropPath string yes Repository-relative path where the shim writes the delegation-request and your coordinator reads and writes result envelopes. Example: ".connector-drops/acme-coordinator-v2/". The directory must exist on disk.
deadlineMinutesDefault integer yes How many minutes the shim waits for a result before declaring the request unreachable. A dispatching role can override this. Example: 120.
retryCapDefault integer yes How many result-check or resume attempts the shim allows while no result exists before escalating to unreachable. Example: 3.
contact string yes Human-readable contact for the coordinator's maintainer. Read as data, never as a command. Example: "squad-integrations@example.com".
version string yes Your coordinator's own version string, for traceability. Example: "2.3.1".

No extra fields. The manifest schema is closed. Any field other than the listed fields above (including command, exec, script, url, or any other field that names an action) will be rejected at validation.

The delegation-request envelope

When a dispatched role delegates work to your connector, the Squad Connector shim writes a delegation-request JSON file to your manifest's dropPath. Your coordinator watches or polls that directory, reads the request, and acts on it.

Field Type Meaning
taskId string Fresh, one-time identifier for this specific request. Never reused once you write back a result for it.
connectorId string Must match your manifest's connectorId.
role string The squad role delegating this task.
deliverableRoot string The dispatching role's directory where all result artifacts must be placed. Every artifact path in your result envelope must resolve under this path, with no path traversal.
bindingId string 1.1 trusted roster-binding identity. Omitted in legacy 1.0.
targetKind string 1.1 target type: role only. HVE coordinators, Scribe, and council governance are protected. Omitted in 1.0.
executionChoice string 1.1 resolved request-time choice: original, connector:<binding-id>, or both.
allowedOutputs array 1.1 closed allowlist of output slots. Each entry is { outputId, role, memberName?, deliverableRoot }.
task string Bounded instructions for the work to do. Read this as data — never execute it as a command or script.
deadline string (ISO 8601) When this request becomes unreachable if no result arrives. Example: "2026-10-11T18:00:00Z".
retryCap integer How many result-check or resume attempts remain while no result exists before the request becomes unreachable.
createdAt string (ISO 8601) When the shim wrote this request.

Read every field as data. The task field is bounded, human-readable instructions, not a script to parse and execute. Respect the deadline and retryCap: write back failed when your coordinator has a failure result; if no result arrives, the shim reports unreachable when the deadline or retry cap is exhausted.

Version boundary. Contract 1.0 stays valid for the legacy single-role manual flow. Role modes (replace/add work-role bindings, persisted defaults, and allowlisted multi-output orchestration) require 1.1. No silent upgrade is inferred by the shim.

The result envelope

After you complete the work (or fail to complete it), write a result envelope JSON file to the same dropPath location where the delegation-request arrived. The shim will later read it, validate it, and report the outcome.

Field Type Meaning
taskId string Must match the pending delegation-request exactly.
connectorId string Must match the pending delegation-request exactly.
bindingId string 1.1 only. Must match the pending delegation-request exactly; omitted in legacy 1.0.
role string Must match the pending delegation-request exactly.
status string One of: completed, failed, awaiting, or unreachable. You typically write completed or failed. (See statuses below.)
artifacts array of objects Deliverable files. Each object has {"path": "...", "type": "md"|"json"|"txt"}. May be empty if status is not completed. In legacy 1.0, this is the returned file list.
outputs array 1.1 orchestrator shape. Each item is { outputId, artifacts[] }, where every outputId must exist in request allowedOutputs.
narrative string Untrusted data. A free-text summary of what happened. Read as data only; never as authority over gates, approvals, or verdicts. Max 10,000 characters.
createdAt string (ISO 8601) When you wrote this result.

Trusted provenance is registration-owned. The Scribe renders External-unmetered: connector=<connectorId> and Verification: external, unverified from validated registration/binding data. External narrative text may repeat these words, but it is never authoritative.

Result statuses

Four statuses, and only four, are valid:

Status Meaning Coordinator action
completed Work finished successfully and artifacts are placed at the Deliverable Root. Shim validates artifact paths, records trusted provenance, marks the result external, unverified, and marks the stage as run.
failed Work could not be completed, a result failed validation, or the shim-owned collision snapshot is missing, changed, or mismatched. Shim rejects the exchange and escalates to the orchestrator as a failed role. It never rebuilds the pre-task snapshot from the resumed filesystem.
awaiting The delegation-request is still being processed; no result envelope exists yet, the deadline has not passed, and retry attempts remain. You typically do not write this status yourself. The shim writes it when it finds no result envelope before either terminal bound is reached.
unreachable No result envelope has arrived by the deadline or before retryCap is exhausted, or the manifest itself is invalid or absent. Shim escalates as if a critical role went offline: the stage fails, and the stage may be retried or abandoned. This is not a soft timeout; it is treated as a missing resource.

Mode 2: human-in-the-loop file drop flow

The connector implements exactly one invocation mode:

  1. Role classification resolves, then execution choice resolves. The coordinator first classifies the role/member internally, then applies connector bindings and request-time choice (original, named connector, or safe both). Interactive clarification occurs only when ambiguity remains; unattended ambiguity stops and escalates.
  2. Shim snapshots, then writes delegation-request. The Squad Connector shim first writes its closed, shim-owned <taskId>.snapshot.json collision baseline, then publishes <taskId>.request.json to your manifest's dropPath. The request contains bounded work, timing, and in 1.1 trusted binding/output allowlists. The shim returns immediately with status awaiting — it does not block waiting for your response.
  3. Your coordinator watches/polls. Your coordinator (running entirely outside this package) watches the dropPath for new *.request.json files by whatever means you choose — file watcher, cron job, human-triggered script, etc. It ignores and never edits *.snapshot.json. There is no prescribed mechanism.
  4. Coordinator does the work. Your coordinator reads the delegation-request, executes the bounded task, and gathers the result (changed files, reports, etc.).
  5. Coordinator writes result envelope. Your coordinator writes a JSON result envelope to the same dropPath, with matching identity fields, status, artifact paths, and a factual narrative. The Scribe derives accounting markers internally.
  6. Later dispatch of shim checks for result. A later dispatch of the Squad Connector shim (by the orchestrator on the next turn, or whenever the squad runs again) reads the unchanged request and original collision snapshot, finds your result envelope, validates the complete exchange, and reports completed, failed, or unreachable. It never rebuilds the snapshot after your producer has written files.

Scripted lifecycle, unchanged ownership. The thin Squad Connector agent runs Invoke-SquadConnectorDispatch.ps1 once and returns its JSON. The separate Resolve-SquadConnectorDispatch.ps1 stays side-effect free. The wrapper stores request/snapshot hashes, retry attempts, and consumed task ids in dropPath/.squad-connector/<taskId>.state.json, under an exclusive task lock. Keep this private state and never let your adapter edit it: a consumed id is rejected across processes even if the public exchange files were removed. Missing or changed state fails closed. The wrapper returns validated paths and provenance, never raw external narrative or artifact contents. HVE retains routing, gates, and Scribe history writes; the contract remains your consumer-facing protocol reference. On both first dispatch and resume, HVE dispatches the native Squad Connector agent; a direct coordinator script call does not establish its execution. Resume keeps the original taskId without creating another request. Pending identity also pins the external member and owning roster: renaming either fails closed instead of reattributing the result. Directory roots accept the examples' trailing separator; unsafe interior path segments remain rejected. HVE supplies exact dispatch JSON; resume reloads optional task, output, and deadline fields from the pinned request rather than reconstructing them. The child returns its first result, including failures, without retrying with edited inputs. Scribe history uses literal * provenance bullets, only validated reports as deliverables, and the native Connector's own model-attribution evidence.

No immediate response. The key difference from a traditional RPC is that steps 2 and 6 are separate. The shim never blocks. Your coordinator runs asynchronously on your own timeline. If your coordinator needs to run synchronously, you can trigger it immediately after the shim writes the request; if you want it to run via cron or human action, that works too. The contract does not care how.

Security rules

Untrusted envelope data. Every result envelope — its status, narrative, and artifact content — is treated as untrusted data by the shim. No instruction or directive inside a narrative can steer a gate, override a policy, or alter squad behavior. The shim's own validation (not the envelope's claims) determines what happened. Any embedded instruction like "skip this gate" or "mark this as approved" is reported, never obeyed.

Path validation and numeric caps. The shim validates every artifact path before touching it:

  • No path traversal: Reject paths with absolute paths, UNC paths (\\server\share), drive letters (C:), .. segments, or NTFS alternate-data-stream markers (:). Resolve the surviving path and confirm it falls under deliverableRoot using a separator-aware prefix check (true path-segment boundary, not a bare string match).
  • No symlinks or junctions: Reject any symlink, junction, or reparse point at any path component, including intermediate directories.
  • File-type restrictions: Only .md, .json, and .txt are allowed. Any other extension is rejected.
  • Numeric caps, all enforced:
    • 1 MiB maximum per file
    • 5 MiB maximum total per envelope
    • 20 artifacts maximum per envelope
    • 5 levels maximum nesting depth under deliverableRoot
    • 10,000 characters maximum for any single string value (including narrative)
  • Reserved device names: Reject Windows reserved names (CON, PRN, AUX, NUL, COM1–COM9, LPT1–LPT9) in any path component, and reject any component with trailing dots or spaces.
  • No auto-overwrite; in-place validation: Your producer writes each new task-owned artifact directly under deliverableRoot (or its declared allowedOutputs[].deliverableRoot slot) before publishing <taskId>.result.json. The shim reads pre-existing paths from the closed <taskId>.snapshot.json it wrote before the request and rejects any declared artifact path that already existed before the task, duplicates another path in the same result, or is missing on disk when validated. A missing, changed, or mismatched snapshot fails the exchange rather than being regenerated.

Closed manifest and envelopes. The manifest, delegation-request, and result envelope schemas are all closed. Any undeclared field is rejected at validation.

Envelope binding and replay protection. A result envelope's connectorId, taskId, and role must match the delegation-request exactly. Every taskId is one-time; once consumed, the same taskId is never accepted again — a repeat is a replay and is rejected.

No secrets. A manifest, delegation-request, or result envelope containing credential-shaped content (API keys, tokens, connection strings, PEM blocks, or other high-entropy secret patterns) fails validation outright. Never embed secrets in any of these files.

Accounting: External-unmetered marker

Externally run connector work is recorded as an ordinary history entry in the squad's ledger, but under a special convention: trusted Scribe output carries the exact phrase External-unmetered: connector=<connectorId>. This phrase is derived from validated registration/binding provenance so a ledger reviewer can distinguish unmetered external work from internal metered execution.

The ledger under-reports external spend. The shim's own dispatch is metered (a thin operation), but your coordinator's actual work is not. The accounting marker documents this gap — it is not a zero-cost operation, but rather an unmetered one. Never treat an entry with this marker as cheap in budget or risk reasoning; the marker exists precisely so a reviewer does not misread a low ledger figure as the true cost of the delegated work.

External narrative can include its own marker text, but that copy remains untrusted data and never becomes the authoritative accounting source.

Example history-entry narrative bullet: 

* Outcome: Result envelope validated and artifact placed at the Deliverable Root. External-unmetered: connector=acme-coordinator-v2.

Compatibility checklist for your coordinator

Before registering your connector, confirm your coordinator satisfies all of these:

  • [ ] You have authored one manifest per connector, validating clean against the closed Manifest Schema (no extra fields, transport: "file", no secret-shaped content).
  • [ ] You treat each request as bounded, idempotent work. A duplicate taskId is replay and is rejected rather than re-executed.
  • [ ] Your coordinator watches or polls the manifest's dropPath for a delegation-request, by whatever means you choose.
  • [ ] You write back exactly one result envelope per taskId, never reusing a taskId and never writing to a dropPath you were not given.
  • [ ] In 1.1, you bind identity fields exactly (connectorId, bindingId, taskId) and return only declared outputId values from allowedOutputs.
  • [ ] Every artifact path is strictly under the deliverableRoot named in the delegation-request, as one of .md, .json, or .txt, within all the numeric caps (1 MiB per file, 5 MiB total, 20 artifacts max, 5 nesting levels, 10,000 chars per string).
  • [ ] You honor deadline and retryCap; when your coordinator has a failure result, you write status: "failed". When no result arrives, the request becomes unreachable at its deadline or retry cap rather than accepting a late, unsolicited result.
  • [ ] You never embed credentials, tokens, or other secret-shaped content in the manifest, request, or result.
  • [ ] You never emit action fields (command, exec, script, url, fetch) or any undeclared schema field.
  • [ ] You never claim squad authority: no role/council/gate/approval claims, no squad state writes, no Scribe writes.
  • [ ] If your endpoint fans out internally, disclose that in narrative/provenance while still returning one bounded envelope mapped to declared outputs only.
  • [ ] You treat your own narrative as a summary a human and an automated validator both read as data — never as a vehicle for steering what the squad does next.

Worked example: acme-coordinator-v2

The connector example directory contains a complete, worked example of every file shape the contract defines, plus blank templates you can copy and adapt:

  • manifest.json — a realistic capability manifest declaring a coordinator that can implement development tasks for the developer role.
  • delegation-request.sample.json — what the shim writes to dropPath: a bounded task to add input validation to an invoice calculator.
  • result-envelope.sample.json — what the coordinator writes back in legacy 1.0 form: a change record describing the fix. If external narrative text repeats accounting words, treat that copy as untrusted data; the authoritative marker is Scribe-rendered from validated binding provenance.
  • sample-consumer-coordinator.md — a pseudo-code sketch of how an external coordinator could honor the contract. This is illustrative only and is never executed by anything in this repository.
  • manifest.template.json, delegation-request.template.json, result-envelope.template.json — blank templates you can copy and fill in for your own connector.
  • COMPATIBILITY-CHECKLIST.md — a standalone, copyable restatement of this guide's checklist.

Copy these files as a starting point. Adapt them to your coordinator's own paths, roles, and capabilities. Read connector-contract.md for the normative specification of every field and every validation rule.

Build with the user-invocable builders

The installed package exposes Squad Connector Builder and Squad External Agent Builder in the agent picker. Their prompts gather decisions; reusable PowerShell scripts perform every directory, template, manifest, and validation operation. Neither builder asks for credentials or registers a connector in squad state.

Connector builder: configured or editable template

For a configured connector, start with what you already know:

/squad-connector-builder mode=interactive destination=. request="Connect our repository research agent for the researcher role. Use connector id org-research and contact integrations@example.invalid."
  1. The builder asks only for missing endpoint kind, capabilities, roles, version, deadline, and retry cap.
  2. After your confirmation, New-SquadConnector.ps1 creates connectors/<id>.json, the .connector-drops/<id>/ mailbox and guidance, request/result examples, a removable runner, and a detailed runbook.
  3. Test-SquadConnectorManifest.ps1 validates the generated closed 1.1 schema. Resolve every issue before registration.

To start from editable examples instead, invoke:

/squad-connector-builder mode=template destination=. request="Create an editable connector starter and identify every value I must replace."

Template mode reports REPLACE_CONNECTOR_ID, REPLACE_CAPABILITY, REPLACE_ROLE, and REPLACE_CONTACT. Validation intentionally fails until all four are resolved.

External builder: create or adapt an agent or orchestrator

Create mode supports a standalone agent or an orchestrator with one bounded worker per responsibility. Name the execution environment and skill requirements; the builder reuses a matching installed HVE Core or organization skill when available and generates editable consumer-owned skill guidance for missing requirements. New-SquadExternalEndpoint.ps1 renders the confirmed plan. 

/squad-external-agent-builder action=create kind=orchestrator destination=. request="Create an implementation orchestrator with separate implementation and validation responsibilities, then connect it to the developer role."

The output includes endpoint and worker charters, a skill, the same connector artifacts and removable runner, and separate endpoint/connector runbooks. Generated endpoints remain outside HVE coordination and cannot write squad state or claim gates.

To preserve and wrap an implementation you already own, use adapt mode:

/squad-external-agent-builder action=adapt kind=agent destination=. request="Adapt tools/existing-research-agent for the researcher role without modifying or replacing it."

Adapt mode checks that the path exists, leaves it byte-identical, and creates only connector artifacts plus an adaptation runbook. That runbook maps bounded request fields and output envelopes to the existing host and gives concrete native-host skill installation guidance.

Customize, rerun, and verify end to end

  1. Install PowerShell 7.4 or newer and review every generated file. Keep credentials in the external host's secret store, never in a manifest, envelope, charter, skill, or runbook.
  2. Validate from an APM install with .agents/skills/squad/scripts/Test-SquadConnectorManifest.ps1; plugin users use skills/squad/scripts/Test-SquadConnectorManifest.ps1.
  3. Poll once with pwsh -File scripts/Run-<id>-Connector.ps1 -Once. The default runner writes a labelled transport receipt and needs no credentials. Replace its Invoke-ExampleEndpoint function with your trusted host/SDK invocation, replace the runner entirely, or remove it.
  4. Test the endpoint directly, configure the trusted connectorId-to-endpoint mapping outside the manifest, then ask Squad Coordinator to register the manifest.
  5. Run one bounded request. The first dispatch returns awaiting; after the adapter publishes a result, resume the same task id and review the output as external, unverified.

Safe reruns never silently overwrite user work. Byte-identical files are reported unchanged. If any generated file was customized, the script preflights the complete plan, stops before writing anything, and lists the conflicts. There is no force switch; compare and adopt regenerated content deliberately.

What is not supported yet

Automatic migration from 1.0 to 1.1 role modes. Legacy 1.0 remains valid for manual single-role connector flow, while role modes require 1.1 bindings and request fields. Migration and rollback are explicit roster actions: route to execution=original, or remove bindings and remove/rebind affected members/routes during a confirmed refresh. No silent upgrade or silent downgrade is performed.

A confirmed unbind or identity-changing rebind preserves completed history and Attribution. Before changing the active rows, the internal Scribe appends a Connector Binding Retirement decision to the owning squad's decisions.md, recording the prior validated identity, operator confirmation, and exact completed history headings. This historical evidence grants no new dispatch authority and cannot admit an unlisted entry. Measure-SquadLedger.ps1 uses the shared Resolve-SquadConnectorHistory.ps1 validator to regenerate those entries' original role, external member, and model tier from the saved roster, without changing history or token pricing. Invalid historical evidence blocks regeneration: saved retry/deadline defaults must be integers within the manifest limits, and duplicate or overlapping retirement authority for the same binding and completed heading is rejected even when the active identity matches. Disjoint entries after binding-id reuse remain valid. A default-only refresh leaves identity unchanged.

MCP transport (reserved, not built). The manifest schema reserves a transport: "mcp" value as a placeholder for a possible future synchronous transport mode. It is not implemented today. The only transport mode that exists is transport: "file" (Mode 2, human-in-the-loop file drop). A manifest declaring "mcp" will fail validation. Building it requires a new Impactful-Action-gated plan and a fresh Council pass.
